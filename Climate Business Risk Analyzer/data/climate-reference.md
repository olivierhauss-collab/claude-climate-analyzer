# Climate Science Reference — Greenly Risk Analyzer

## Temperature Scenario Overview

### +2C Pathway (IPCC AR6 SSP1-2.6)
Global mean surface temperature increase of 1.5C reached between 2030-2035, stabilizing near 2.0C by 2100. Requires global CO2 emissions to decline 25% by 2030 and reach net-zero by 2070. Cumulative carbon budget remaining from 2020: ~1,150 GtCO2 for 67% probability of staying below 2C. Sea level rise: 0.32-0.62m by 2100. Extreme heat events that occurred once per 50 years in pre-industrial climate occur 13.9 times per 50 years at +2C. Heavy precipitation events increase by 14% in intensity at +2C globally.

### +3C Pathway (IPCC AR6 SSP2-4.5 / SSP3-7.0 trajectory)
Current policies trajectory reaches +2.5-2.9C by 2100 (Climate Action Tracker 2023). With pledged NDCs: +2.4-2.6C. At +3C: sea level rise 0.44-0.76m by 2100, continuing for centuries. Extreme heat events increase to 39.2 times per 50 years. Arctic sea ice free in summer multiple times per decade. Amazon rainforest faces dieback tipping point above 2.5C. Coral reefs decline by >99% at +3C versus 70-90% at +2C. Permafrost thaw releases 14-175 GtC by 2100 under high warming.

### Economic Damage
Kotz et al. (2024, Nature): committed global income reduction of ~19% by 2049 relative to no-warming baseline, regardless of mitigation pathway chosen now. Damages driven primarily by temperature variability and extreme precipitation. Burke et al. (2015): unmitigated warming reduces global GDP 23% by 2100 relative to no warming. NGFS estimates cumulative GDP loss of 5-13% by 2050 under hot house scenario. Swiss Re sigma: global insured losses from natural catastrophes reached $108B in 2023, up from $40B annual average in 2010s. Munich Re: overall economic losses from weather events averaged $250B/year 2019-2023.

## Physical Risk Reference by Region

### Sea Level Rise
- Global baseline: 3.7mm/year (2006-2018), accelerating from 1.3mm/year (1901-1971).
- Most affected: Small Island Developing States, Bangladesh (17% land area below 1m), Netherlands, Vietnam (Mekong Delta), US Gulf Coast, Egypt (Nile Delta).
- +2C: 0.32-0.62m by 2100. 410M people exposed to coastal flooding annually by 2100.
- +3C: 0.44-0.76m by 2100. Risk of partial West Antarctic Ice Sheet collapse raises upper bound to 1.5m.

### Extreme Heat
- Global baseline: 2023 was hottest year on record at +1.48C above pre-industrial. 6.7B person-days of extreme heat exposure in 2023.
- Most affected: South Asia (India, Pakistan), Middle East (Persian Gulf), Sub-Saharan Africa, Southeast Asia, Southern Europe.
- +2C: 2.6 billion people exposed to severe heat at least once every 5 years. Labor productivity loss: 2.5% of global working hours.
- +3C: 4.2 billion people regularly exposed. Wet-bulb temperatures exceed 35C threshold in parts of South Asia and Persian Gulf, making outdoor work lethal. Labor productivity loss: 5.3% of global working hours.

### Drought
- Global baseline: drought-affected land area has doubled since 1970s.
- Most affected: Mediterranean, Southern Africa, Central Asia, Western US, Australia, Brazil (Cerrado).
- +2C: 410 million people exposed to severe drought. Soil moisture declines 10-20% in Mediterranean.
- +3C: 700+ million people exposed. Breadbasket regions face simultaneous crop failures with 4.5% probability per decade.

### Flooding (River and Pluvial)
- Global baseline: flood frequency has increased 4x since 1980. Annual flood damages: $82B average (2010-2022).
- Most affected: China (Yangtze, Pearl River), India (Ganges-Brahmaputra), Western Europe (Rhine, Meuse), Southeast Asia, East Africa.
- +2C: global flood risk increases 170% for population exposed to 100-year floods.
- +3C: flood risk increases 300%. 1-in-100-year floods become 1-in-20-year events across Central Europe and East Asia.

### Wildfire
- Global baseline: area burned has increased 2x in western US since 1984. 2023 Canadian wildfires burned 18.5 million hectares. Global wildfire emissions: 6.6 GtCO2 in 2023.
- Most affected: Western US, Canada, Australia, Mediterranean Europe, Siberia, Amazon/Cerrado.
- +2C: fire weather season lengthens by 18-20 days globally. Area burned increases 35% in Mediterranean.
- +3C: fire weather season lengthens 30+ days. Boreal forest fires increase 150%. Pyroconvective events (fire-generated thunderstorms) become routine.

### Tropical Cyclones
- Global baseline: proportion of Category 4-5 cyclones increased 25-30% since 1979. Annual cyclone damages: $78B average (2000-2023).
- Most affected: Philippines, Japan, Southeast US, Caribbean, Mozambique, Bangladesh, Vietnam.
- +2C: intensity increases 5-10%. Rainfall rates within cyclones increase 14%. Poleward migration expands risk to previously unaffected coasts.
- +3C: proportion of Cat 4-5 increases by 13%. Peak wind speeds increase 5-10%. Rapid intensification events become 2x more frequent.

## Sector Impact Lookup Table

### Section A — Agriculture, Forestry and Fishing
- Physical risks: crop yield loss (maize -6% per degree, wheat -5%), heat stress on livestock reducing productivity 10-25% at +2C, shifting growing seasons, pollinator decline, aquaculture disruption from ocean warming and acidification.
- Transition risks: methane pricing (agriculture = 10% of global GHG), fertilizer regulation (N2O), land-use mandates, deforestation bans, EU CBAM on embedded emissions.
- Supply chain: irrigation water scarcity, seed supply concentration, cold chain disruption, port closures affecting exports.
- Opportunities: precision agriculture, drought-resistant cultivars, regenerative agriculture carbon credits ($15-50/tCO2e), vertical farming, alternative proteins.

### Section B — Mining and Quarrying
- Physical risks: water scarcity for extraction processes, extreme heat reducing worker output 15-30%, flooding of open-pit mines, permafrost thaw destabilizing Arctic operations.
- Transition risks: demand decline for thermal coal (IEA NZE: -90% by 2050), stranded fossil fuel reserves ($1-4 trillion at risk), tightening ESG finance standards.
- Supply chain: critical mineral demand surge (lithium +42x, cobalt +21x by 2040 under IEA NZE), geopolitical concentration of rare earths.
- Opportunities: critical mineral extraction for energy transition, mine-site renewable energy, circular economy metals recycling.

### Section C — Manufacturing
- Physical risks: factory flooding (40% of global manufacturing in flood-prone areas), heat-related equipment failures, supply chain disruption from extreme events.
- Transition risks: EU CBAM (effective 2026), Scope 3 reporting mandates, material efficiency requirements, product carbon footprint labeling (EU Battery Regulation).
- Supply chain: 60% of raw materials sourced from climate-vulnerable regions, semiconductor fabs concentrated in typhoon-exposed Taiwan, just-in-time models amplify disruption.
- Opportunities: green steel (hydrogen DRI), low-carbon cement (CCUS), circular manufacturing, energy efficiency retrofits, heat pump production.

### Section D — Electricity, Gas, Steam and Air Conditioning Supply
- Physical risks: thermal power plant efficiency drops 0.5-1% per degree of warming, hydropower output declines 20-40% in drought-affected basins, grid infrastructure damage from storms, transmission losses increase with temperature.
- Transition risks: coal plant stranding (IEA NZE: unabated coal ends by 2040), carbon pricing exposure ($50-150/tCO2 by 2030 in major markets), renewable portfolio standards.
- Supply chain: solar panel manufacturing concentration (China 80%), rare earth dependency for wind turbines, battery mineral supply bottlenecks.
- Opportunities: renewable energy deployment (solar costs fell 89% 2010-2023), grid-scale battery storage, green hydrogen, smart grid infrastructure, distributed energy.

### Section E — Water Supply, Sewerage, Waste Management
- Physical risks: water stress affects 2 billion people currently, projected to affect 3.5 billion by 2050. Infrastructure flooding, combined sewer overflows increase 20-40% at +2C.
- Transition risks: stricter effluent standards, methane capture mandates for landfills, extended producer responsibility regulations.
- Supply chain: energy-intensive desalination dependency, chemical supply for water treatment, aging infrastructure replacement.
- Opportunities: water recycling technology, smart metering, desalination powered by renewables, waste-to-energy, biogas from wastewater.

### Section F — Construction
- Physical risks: extreme heat reduces outdoor labor productivity 20-30% in summer months, flooding delays increase project costs 5-15%, material degradation from temperature cycling.
- Transition risks: building energy codes tightening (EU Energy Performance of Buildings Directive: all new buildings near-zero by 2030), embodied carbon regulations, retrofit mandates.
- Supply chain: cement (8% of global CO2), steel (7%), timber price volatility from wildfire and deforestation policy.
- Opportunities: green building certification market ($500B by 2030), energy-efficient retrofits, mass timber construction, building-integrated renewables.

### Section G — Wholesale and Retail Trade
- Physical risks: supply chain disruption from extreme weather (30% of global trade routes climate-exposed), cold chain failures, warehouse flooding, consumer demand shifts.
- Transition risks: Scope 3 emissions disclosure, product carbon footprint labeling, single-use packaging bans, circular economy mandates.
- Supply chain: agricultural commodity price volatility (+20-30% under drought conditions), shipping disruption (Panama Canal drought reduced throughput 36% in 2023).
- Opportunities: sustainable product lines (28% premium willingness), circular economy services, local sourcing, last-mile electrification.

### Section H — Transportation and Storage
- Physical risks: road buckling from heat (+60% maintenance costs at +2C), port flooding, flight disruption from turbulence (+100-200% clear-air turbulence at +2C), rail buckling above 35C.
- Transition risks: EU ETS for shipping (2024) and aviation, ICE vehicle phase-outs (EU 2035), sustainable aviation fuel mandates (EU ReFuelEU: 6% by 2030, 70% by 2050).
- Supply chain: fuel price exposure (transport = 23% of energy-related CO2), EV charging infrastructure gaps, alternative fuel availability.
- Opportunities: fleet electrification, autonomous logistics, hydrogen heavy transport, rail modal shift, sustainable aviation fuel production.

### Section I — Accommodation and Food Service
- Physical risks: tourism destination degradation (coral bleaching, snow loss: Alps ski season shortened 30 days by 2050), extreme heat deterring visitors, water scarcity in tourist regions.
- Transition risks: food waste regulations, single-use plastic bans, energy efficiency mandates for buildings, animal agriculture carbon pricing.
- Supply chain: food commodity price volatility, seafood supply decline (marine catch potential -8% at +2C, -16% at +3C), local sourcing challenges.
- Opportunities: agritourism, plant-based menus, energy-efficient hospitality, climate-resilient destination development.

### Section J — Information and Communication
- Physical risks: data center cooling costs increase 10-20% per degree, submarine cable exposure to sea-level rise, extreme weather disrupting cell towers and fiber infrastructure.
- Transition risks: data center energy regulation (EU Energy Efficiency Directive), e-waste regulations, Scope 2/3 reporting.
- Supply chain: semiconductor supply concentration in climate-vulnerable Taiwan and South Korea, rare earth dependency, energy grid reliability.
- Opportunities: climate analytics software, digital twin modeling, remote work infrastructure, AI-driven energy optimization, carbon accounting platforms.

### Section K — Financial Activities and Insurance
- Physical risks: portfolio exposure to climate-affected assets, mortgage defaults in flood zones, insurance claims escalation (combined ratio deterioration of 5-15pp in exposed markets).
- Transition risks: stranded asset write-downs ($1-4T fossil fuel assets at risk), climate stress testing requirements (ECB, BoE, Fed), fiduciary duty interpretation expanding.
- Supply chain: data quality for climate risk modeling, reinsurance cost escalation (property catastrophe reinsurance rates +30% 2022-2024).
- Opportunities: green bonds (issuance >$500B/year), climate risk analytics, transition finance, parametric insurance, ESG integration advisory.

### Section L — Real Estate Activities
- Physical risks: property value decline in flood zones (5-15% discount observed), heat island effect in urban areas (+2-5C vs rural), storm damage to building stock.
- Transition risks: Minimum Energy Performance Standards (EU: all buildings EPC C by 2033), carbon tax on building operations, mandatory climate risk disclosure for property transactions.
- Supply chain: building materials cost volatility, skilled labor shortage for retrofits, insurance availability withdrawal in high-risk zones.
- Opportunities: green building premium (5-10% rental, 10-20% sale), energy retrofit financing, climate-resilient urban development.

### Section Q — Human Health and Social Work
- Physical risks: heat-related mortality increases 2.5x at +2C, vector-borne disease expansion (dengue risk area +20% at +2C), air quality deterioration from wildfire smoke and ozone.
- Transition risks: healthcare system decarbonization mandates (NHS net zero by 2040), pharmaceutical supply chain scrutiny, energy-intensive facility regulations.
- Supply chain: pharmaceutical manufacturing in climate-vulnerable regions (India: 60% of global generic production), cold chain for vaccines, medical supply disruption.
- Opportunities: climate-health surveillance systems, telemedicine, heatwave early warning systems, air quality monitoring.

## Quantified Reference Data

### NGFS Phase V Scenarios (2024)
- Orderly Transition (Net Zero 2050): carbon price reaches $160/tCO2 by 2030, $300+ by 2050. Warming limited to 1.5-1.8C. GDP impact: -2 to -4% by 2050 relative to baseline. Stranded assets concentrated in fossil fuels.
- Disorderly Transition: delayed action then sudden policy shift. Carbon price spikes to $400+/tCO2 by 2035. GDP impact: -5 to -8% by 2050. Financial market volatility, abrupt repricing of carbon-intensive assets.
- Hot House World (Current Policies): minimal transition effort. Warming reaches 2.5-3.0C by 2100. Physical damage GDP impact: -10 to -23% by 2100. Chronic damages accelerate post-2050. Tipping points triggered.

### IEA Net Zero 2050 Milestones
- 2025: no new unabated coal plants approved, universal energy access.
- 2030: 60% of global car sales are EVs, tripling of renewable capacity, phasedown of unabated coal in advanced economies.
- 2035: all new buildings zero-carbon-ready, electricity sector net-zero in advanced economies.
- 2040: 50% of heavy truck sales are electric, unabated coal completely phased out, 50% of existing buildings retrofitted.
- 2050: 90% of electricity from renewables, nearly all cars on road are EVs, heavy industry CCUS at scale.

### Carbon Pricing Projections
- EU ETS: traded at 55-95 EUR/tCO2 in 2023-2024. Projected: 100-150 EUR by 2030. CBAM fully operational 2026.
- Global coverage: 23% of global GHG emissions covered by carbon pricing in 2024 (World Bank).
- High Ambition Coalition for Nature and People: push for $150/tCO2 minimum by 2030.
- IMF recommendation: $75/tCO2 global floor by 2030 for Paris alignment.
- Voluntary carbon markets: $2B in 2023, projected $50B by 2030.

### Stranded Asset Estimates
- Carbon Tracker: $1 trillion in upstream oil and gas assets at risk under 1.5C pathway.
- IRENA: $3.6 trillion in fossil fuel assets could become stranded by 2050.
- Real estate: $2.4 trillion in global property value at risk from sea-level rise alone (Hallegatte et al.).
- Auto industry: ICE powertrain manufacturing assets at risk as EV mandates accelerate.

## TCFD Risk Taxonomy

### Physical Risks — Acute
- Tropical cyclones and severe storms: wind damage, storm surge, business interruption. Frequency of Cat 4-5 increasing.
- Flooding (fluvial, pluvial, coastal): asset damage, supply chain disruption. Losses doubled since 2000.
- Wildfire: direct property destruction, air quality impacts, evacuation disruption. Season lengthening globally.
- Extreme heat events: worker safety, equipment failure, energy demand spikes. Heatwave frequency tripled since 1980s.

### Physical Risks — Chronic
- Sea level rise: permanent coastal inundation, saltwater intrusion, infrastructure undermining. Committed to 0.3-0.6m even with aggressive mitigation.
- Mean temperature increase: crop yield shifts, cooling demand increase (3-5% per degree), ecosystem migration.
- Precipitation pattern changes: water availability redistribution, agricultural zone shifts, hydropower variability.
- Ocean acidification: pH decline of 0.1 units since pre-industrial (26% acidity increase), shellfish and coral reef degradation.

### Transition Risks — Regulatory
- Carbon pricing (ETS, carbon tax): direct cost on emissions. EU ETS, UK ETS, China ETS covering 23% of global emissions.
- Emissions standards: vehicle emission limits, industrial emission caps, building energy codes.
- Disclosure mandates: EU CSRD (50,000+ companies), SEC climate rules, ISSB standards adopted by 20+ jurisdictions.
- Land use restrictions: deforestation regulations (EU Deforestation Regulation 2024), wetland protection.

### Transition Risks — Technological
- Low-carbon substitution: green hydrogen replacing grey ($2-4/kg vs $1-2/kg in 2024, parity expected 2028-2032), EV cost parity reached 2024 for many segments.
- Legacy asset obsolescence: coal plants, ICE vehicle factories, gas pipeline networks.
- R&D reallocation pressure: companies spending <2% revenue on green R&D face competitive disadvantage.

### Transition Risks — Market
- Consumer preference shifts: 66% of consumers willing to pay more for sustainable products (NielsenIQ 2023).
- Commodity price volatility: fossil fuel demand peak expected 2025-2030 (IEA), critical mineral price spikes.
- Investor ESG pressure: $35 trillion in ESG AUM (2024), net-zero asset owner alliance covering $10 trillion.

### Transition Risks — Reputational
- Greenwashing litigation: 2,000+ climate-related court cases globally by 2024.
- Stakeholder activism: shareholder climate resolutions increasing 25% year-over-year.
- Talent attraction: 70% of millennials prefer employers with strong sustainability commitments.

### Opportunities
- Resource efficiency: energy efficiency improvements yield 20-30% cost savings in buildings, 15-25% in industrial processes.
- Energy source: renewable PPAs lock in lower long-term costs. Corporate renewable procurement reached 50 GW in 2023.
- Products and services: global green technology market projected at $9.5 trillion by 2030. Climate adaptation market: $2 trillion/year by 2030.
- Markets: new geographic markets from shifting agricultural zones, Arctic shipping routes, climate migration destinations.
- Resilience: business continuity investment reduces climate disruption costs 3-5x. Insurance premium reductions for adapted assets 10-25%.
